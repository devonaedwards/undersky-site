Published 10 August 2026 by Pacefyx, Inc. We make Undersky, which is one of the alternatives below. We have tried to be straight about who should not switch.

Co-Star is the app that made a generation care about their rising sign, it uses real astronomy data, and the writing has a flat confidence nobody else has matched. Those are real reasons to keep it. Below is what to move to if something specific is bothering you.

Who should stay on Co-Star?

Anyone who uses it to see friends' charts, or who stays for the voice. Those are the two things Co-Star does better than anything else, and no app on this page is a replacement for either.

Astrology is social for a lot of people, and Co-Star is the only one of these built that way from the start. If your friends are all on it, switching costs you the thing you actually use.

Who bought Co-Star, and does it matter?

Midjourney acquired Co-Star, announced on its own site on 23 July 2026. Bloomberg reported the next day that Co-Star had around 4.3 million monthly active users, citing Sensor Tower. Co-Star's founder and her team joined Midjourney.

This does not change how the app works today, and it is not a reason on its own to leave. It is worth knowing because whoever owns an app also has whatever that app collected, and apps change hands. That is a general point about handing over data, not a criticism of either company.

If that thought is what brought you here, the useful move is not to switch apps on vibes. It is to read the privacy section on each app's store page and decide from what is actually declared. We wrote a short guide to doing that, including the one thing those labels do not cover.
