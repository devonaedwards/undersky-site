An astrology app that never asks your gender

Published 10 August 2026 by Pacefyx, Inc. We make Undersky, so this is a page about our own app.

Undersky never asks your gender. It has no field for it. A chart is made from a date, a time and a place. Those are the three things Undersky asks for, and then it gets on with reading the sky.

This was not a setting somebody added later. There was never a box, so there was nothing to go back and take out.

What Undersky asks you for

Your birth date, your birth time and your birth place. That is the whole form. There is no account, no email, no title, no pronoun picker, and no second screen where the real questions turn up.

Nothing you type into Undersky is a demographic record. A date, a time and a place are the coordinates of a sky. They go into the chart, the chart goes into the reading, and none of it goes anywhere else.

How Undersky handles relationships

By role, never by gender. You say who someone is to you, and the choices are partner, friend, parent and child, siblings, extended family, estranged family and frenemy. Undersky never asks who plays which part.

Two charts are two charts. Undersky reads any pair the same way, because a chart carries no gender for it to read. What changes the reading is the kind of relationship you said it was, which is the thing that actually changes what is worth saying.

You are never asked to explain yourself to Undersky, because Undersky never asks.

Who finds out who you are reading about

Nobody, because Undersky never receives it. Your birth details, the people you add and every reading are worked out on your phone and stay there. There is no account to make and the App Store privacy label for Undersky reads Data Not Collected.

A compatibility feature is the sharpest version of this problem, and it is worth being plain about why. To read two charts together, an app has to know that you are reading about that person. Held on a server, that is a record of who you are seeing, sitting in a database for as long as the company lasts. Worked out on your phone, it is not a record of anything.

A field that does not exist cannot leak, cannot be subpoenaed, and cannot be sold with the company. Companies get bought. Nobody can buy what we never had, and that is a stronger protection than any promise we could make about how carefully we would have looked after it.

If you want to check this rather than take it from us, there is a separate guide to reading any astrology app's privacy label yourself, including the one thing the label does not cover.

Is Undersky in your language?

Undersky is written in 26 languages, and 13 of them are in a script that is not the Latin alphabet. Arabic, which carries both Arabic and Persian. Cyrillic, which carries Russian and Ukrainian. Armenian, Greek, Hebrew, Devanagari, Japanese, Korean, Thai, and Chinese in both Simplified and Traditional.

Your chart is read in your language, in your own script.

Being shipped in a language is not the same as being usable in it, so here is what that took. Every language was written by someone working in that language, against a glossary of the tradition's own terms, and read back by a second independent reviewer. The terms of the tradition stay as they are, because softening a real term into something more comfortable is how you end up with an app that says nothing.

The half that usually breaks is the half you type into. A decimal comma, a Buddhist-era year, digits in your own numerals: get those wrong and the app hands you a chart where every visible word is correct and the birth time is not. Undersky localises the input as carefully as the output. Right-to-left languages are laid out right-to-left, not merely translated.

Are more languages coming?

Yes. We are still adding languages, and one gets added when it can be done properly, by someone working in it. That is the only gate, and it is the reason the list grows slowly.

At the moment Undersky is written in more languages than its own App Store listing advertises, which is an unusual direction for that to be wrong in, and we are fixing it from the listing end.

Why Undersky was built this way

Undersky was thought up in the Bay Area, where you assume as a matter of course that the person next to you might be anyone, and that a form which demands they pick a side is a badly designed form. So the questions never got asked in the first place. There was no diversity review at the end of the project, because there was nothing left to review.

That is also just better engineering. A field you do not collect is a field you cannot get wrong, cannot lose, and never have to defend. The design that respects people the most turned out to be the design with the fewest moving parts, which is a nice thing to be able to report.

If you have ever opened an astrology app and been asked to pick one of two boxes, neither of which was you, and then wondered what it was going to do with the answer: this one does not ask.

Common questions

Does Undersky ask your gender?

No. Undersky has no field for it. A birth chart is made from a date, a time and a place, and those are the only things Undersky asks for.

Does Undersky work for same-sex couples?

Yes, exactly as it works for anyone else. Undersky reads two charts against each other, and a chart carries no gender, so nothing in the calculation or the writing depends on who is which.

How does Undersky describe relationships?

By role: partner, friend, parent and child, siblings, extended family, estranged family, frenemy. None of those has a gender attached to it.

Can anyone find out who you are reading about?

No, because Undersky never receives it. Your people and your readings are worked out on your phone and stay there, and there is no account.

What languages is Undersky written in?

26, with 13 of them in a script that is not the Latin alphabet. Each one was written by someone working in that language rather than machine-translated.
